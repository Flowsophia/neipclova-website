06 / BETTER TASTE

The taste begins
before the plate.

Ten ingredient families. A practical way to discuss what you want to explore.

01

The shared starting point

Water

Water for mixing, cooking, beverages and food-contact processes. Discuss the application and confirm suitability before a trial.

02

Depth, salt and balance

Korean Soy Sauce & Fermented Sauces

Soy sauce, gochujang and doenjang offer different starting points. Compare the same batch, concentration and recipe when evaluating a treatment option.

03

Aroma and texture

Sesame Oil & Olive Oil

Explore aroma, mouthfeel and how an oil carries a recipe. Keep source, quantity, storage and temperature consistent between samples.

04

A powder becomes a structure

Wheat Flour

Bread, noodles, batters and sauces begin with a measured powder. Water, mixing and cooking conditions belong in the comparison too.

05

Texture deserves attention

Potato Starch

Explore thickening and coating applications. A treatment proposal needs to be evaluated alongside the actual recipe and processing conditions.

06

Sweetness with character

Jocheong & Sweet Syrups

Korean grain syrup, other syrups and sugars can be considered through sweetness, aroma and texture—using matched samples.

07

A layered conversation

Curry & Spice Blends

Compare the same spice blend and preparation. Regional curry styles differ; a more intense flavor is not automatically a better result.

08

Familiar sauces, careful comparisons

Ketchup & Mayonnaise

Explore tomato-based sauces and emulsified condiments. Record acidity, texture and sensory preference without assuming a safety or nutritional change.

09

Body, richness and finish

Cheese & Dairy Ingredients

Discuss specific products and storage conditions. Flavor preference does not replace shelf-life or food-safety evaluation.

10

From the growing environment

Fresh Ingredients & Proteins

Vegetables, fruit, meat, poultry, eggs and seafood connect the table to the conditions in which food is produced. Evaluate each application separately.

POWDER · LIQUID · SOLID

Choose the application.
Keep the comparison clear.

01

A powder

Flour, starch or spices enter a recipe through measured mixing and hydration. Identify the ingredient and processing step first.

02

A liquid

Water, oil, soy sauce or syrup can carry a proposed application. Contact suitability, handling conditions and the treatment protocol must be confirmed.

03

A solid

Fresh ingredients may involve cooking, mixing or immersion in a suitable liquid. Ask about the method for the actual food; no universal immersion procedure is specified here.

Neipclova’s proprietary method is discussed individually. “Simple” does not remove the need to confirm hygiene, food-contact suitability and process controls.
