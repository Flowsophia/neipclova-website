TECHNICAL LANGUAGE

Know what you are specifying.

Open a term for its meaning. The enquiry choices below describe your project requirements; they do not imply that every finish meets every requirement.

Substrate

The base receiving the finish: for example MDF, HDF, steel, aluminium or an existing laminate. Its condition affects adhesion and appearance.

MDF / HDF / particleboard

Engineered wood boards. MDF is medium-density fibreboard; HDF is high-density fibreboard. Particleboard contains bonded wood particles. Porous faces and edges need an appropriate preparation system.

PP / PET / OPP

PP means polypropylene. PET means polyethylene terephthalate. OPP means oriented polypropylene. These identify polymer families, not automatic proof of a particular performance.

CMF

Colour, Material and Finish: the combined design specification for visual appearance, texture and gloss.

TLF / thermal lamination

Thermal Laminate Film: a film interface bonded through an industrial heat-and-pressure process. Confirm the grade, temperature window and equipment.

Bonding interface

The adhesive, primer or thermal-lamination layer that joins the decorative surface to its base. Compatibility must be established for both materials.

Peel adhesion / edge lift

Peel adhesion is resistance to separation under a defined test. Edge lift is separation starting at an edge. Test conditions and ageing must accompany the result.

UV / weathering / colour change

UV is ultraviolet radiation. Weathering testing evaluates changes under stated light, heat and moisture exposure. Test duration alone does not establish real-world service life.

VOC / emissions

Volatile organic compounds and other emitted substances. A report must identify the tested construction, method and conditions; film results do not automatically cover the underlying board.

Fire performance

The reaction of a specified material or assembly to fire under a defined test. Specify the required method and assembly; decorative sheets are not universally fireproof.

Abrasion / chemical resistance

Abrasion concerns wear from repeated contact. Chemical resistance concerns defined cleaners or substances at stated concentrations and exposure times.

MOQ / batch / grain direction

MOQ is minimum order quantity. Batch identifies a production lot. Grain direction is the orientation of a wood or linear pattern; align it with the panel layout.

Mock-up / pilot panel

A representative trial installation or manufactured panel used to approve appearance, bonding, seams and edge details before larger-scale work.

Exterior / marine / automotive qualification

Application-specific review of exposure and required tests. Architectural interiors, yacht interiors, vehicle interiors and exterior wraps require separate suitability decisions.

01 / INSTALLATION PLANNING

A disciplined specification.

01

Inspect & prepare

Confirm substrate stability, surface condition and moisture. Clean, repair and level the intended surface before the trial.

02

Confirm the bonding method

Select the appropriate primer or industrial lamination route for the grade and substrate. Follow the approved product instructions.

03

Approve a mock-up

Review colour, texture, edge detail, adhesion and grain direction on a representative panel. Plan consistent production batches.

04

Install & maintain

The supplied decorative-sheet guide gives an application ambient range of 10–38°C. Confirm the final grade-specific conditions and cleaning method before work starts.
